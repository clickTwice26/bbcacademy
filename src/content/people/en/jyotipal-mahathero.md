His Holiness the 10th Sangharaj Jyotipal Mahathero was born on 14 January 1914 and ordained in 1935. From boyhood he was a humanitarian and a patron of education. In his adult years he founded many institutions for human welfare, devoted to religion, education, vocational training and socio-economic development, as well as orphanages.

He was an internationally renowned scholar and a Tripitaka Visharad. His extensive writing, research and findings show how deeply he mastered many subjects. He wrote a large number of books, and many of them are now part of the curriculum in schools, colleges and universities. His research on the Charyapada, the Buddhist mystic songs in early Bengali, has given learners, scholars and historians a clear understanding of our history and culture.

A legendary figure in the history of 20th-century Buddhism in Bangladesh and a scholar of international renown, he was the embodiment of humanity. As a harbinger of peace he was awarded the Asian Peace Gold Medal. For his role as a key international organiser during Bangladesh's Liberation War he received the Independence Award, the highest state honour, and he was also awarded the Ekushey Padak for his contributions to human welfare and social service. He was recognised for global citizenship by the IARF (UK) as a human rights activist, and received the title Agga-Saddhamma-Jotikadhaja from Myanmar, among many other honours and titles at home and abroad.

He travelled many times to America, Canada, China, Japan, Taiwan, Singapore, Vietnam, India, Sri Lanka, Nepal, the Soviet Union, Thailand, Malaysia and Myanmar to take part in Buddhist and interfaith seminars and conferences.

Venerable Jyotipal Mahathero passed away on 12 April 2002 at J. J. Hospital in Mumbai, India. People of all walks of life from home and abroad came to pay their last respects at his funeral, held with a State Guard of Honour on 9 and 10 January 2003 at Baraigaon Kanak Chaitya Buddhist Monastery in Laksam, Cumilla, the holy place he founded in his native village. Ministers, state ministers, members of parliament, diplomats, local administrators and countless devotees attended, and the solemn occasion became a celebration of his life.
