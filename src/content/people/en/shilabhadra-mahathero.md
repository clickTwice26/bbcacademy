Venerable Shilabhadra Mahathero was born on 4 August 1952 in Baraigaon, a remote village in Laksam, Cumilla. On his mother's side he is a descendant of His Holiness the 10th Sangharaj Jyotipal Mahathero, under whose guidance he was ordained in the Theravada tradition on 11 July 1975. He has dedicated his life to the betterment of society and to spreading the Buddha's Theravada teachings.

Through tireless effort he established the Bangladesh Buddhist Cultural Academy at the foot of the ancient Salban Vihara in Kotbari, Mainamati. Under its auspices he went on to undertake a major project: the World Peace Pagoda within the sacred grounds of New Salban Vihara. He also founded the Jyotipal Mahathero Buddhist Orphanage High School at Baraigaon, Laksam, Cumilla. By establishing some 20 Buddhist monasteries, he has helped the neglected indigenous Buddhist communities of North Bengal and the Chittagong Hill Tracts practise their faith, and the fading Buddhist religion and culture of North Bengal are being revived as a result. He is actively involved with various organisations at home and abroad, and he recites the Holy Tripitaka on Bangladesh Radio and Bangladesh Television. He is well known at home and abroad for his service to education, religion and the welfare of humanity.

He has travelled many times to America, Canada, China, Japan, India, Sri Lanka, Nepal, Taiwan, Singapore, Vietnam, Thailand, Malaysia and Myanmar for Buddhist and interfaith seminars and conferences.

As a humanist and missionary monk, he bears overall responsibility for 21 Buddhist monasteries, two orphanages, a high school and a kindergarten. In recognition of this work he has been honoured by the Supreme Sangha Council of Bangladesh (Sangharaj Bhikkhu Mahasabha), the Mother Teresa Welfare Trust and the Department of Social Services.
